    How to play

    Touch: drag left or right on the picture or the steering pad to steer (drag up or down to move in the menu); GAS, BRAKE and BOOST are under the other thumb. GAS also starts from the menu.

    Menu: ↑↓ pick a line, ←→ change it, Enter (or X / Z) starts. Four tracks: Neo City, Blue Deep (the ocean), Gold Wind (a crosswind over the wheat) and Old Port (corners close together, and two hairpins); five strengths of rival, or none (a time trial); race, practice (no laps, no damage), two-player link, or learn (a race against the rivals in which you are never ranked out: for learning a track). Four cars: ARROW the all-rounder; HORNET quick off the mark, grippy and nimble, but hits hurt it more; BISON tough (hits hurt it half as much) but heavier to steer and in a bend; COMET the fastest, slow to get there, and it slides.

    Keys: ←→ steer · X / Space = accelerate · Z or ↓ = brake; with ←→ as well, a skid turn that turns harder, and with the gas held too, a lean (F-Zero's L and R: harder still, and no braking) · ↑ = boost (one per lap); in the air ↑ noses down and ↓ noses up · Shift = engine sound on/off · after a race, Enter = go again and Shift = menu · Enter + Shift together = menu · F = fullscreen · L = the screen's skin · G = the guide · gamepads supported (bottom/left face button or right shoulder = accelerate, right/top face button or left shoulder = brake)

    Each track is a fixed lap of straights and corners, the same every time, so you can learn it: a corner shows as a bend on the horizon a couple of seconds before you reach it. The driving is SNES F-Zero's, its numbers and all: the d-pad swings where the car points, harder the longer you hold it, and the swing carries on a moment after you let go, so let go a little before you are pointing where you want; where the car goes follows where it points while you hold the gas or the steering (let go of both and it slides on the way it was going: a drift). The road turns under the car, and the view turns with it. A bend is taken as fast as the car can turn to follow it: the gentle ones flat out, a hairpin at about three quarters of top speed, so brake before it (the brakes take the edge off and never stop you, and they cut a boost short; off the gas alone the car barely slows). The inside of a bend is the short way round. What is on the road shows up a good second before you reach it. Hit the rail and you glance off it, turned back along the road with the wheel straight, in your hands again (a hard hit takes them for a moment): it slows you and costs health, and at zero the car is gone. Brake for the hairpins, and the two pulsing patches on the left after the start line are the pit: brake down to a crawl over them and they give back about 60% of what you have lost, a little under top speed about half, flat out a third, boosting a sixth (slow down to come out healthier, or blast through to win). Dirt (rough dark patches) slows you to a crawl, and halves your steering. Striped roadblocks are barriers (a hit costs health and knocks you aside); grey patches with flickering arrows are boost pads (cross the middle for the full boost); grey ramps with a dark rim (the tunnel mouth's shape, no arrows) are jumps: drive straight onto one and you fly, with a full boost forward; miss it and nothing happens. There is no steering in the air, so an angle carries you across the road, and the road can turn under you while you fly: nose down (↑) to come down sooner before a bend, nose up (↓) to land gently. Every patch acts just where it is drawn. A mistake lets a rival pounce.

    The finish: past the line your car speeds off up the road while the rivals cruise by, and your place (or OUT) is painted onto the road ahead for them to drive through. Your time, best lap and any record are in the line above the picture.

    Records and your ghost: each track keeps your best lap and best race in this browser, for each car (the menu shows them). Press GHOST and your best race on the track in that car comes back as the other car, frame for frame: the page sets MODE to LINK for it, so press GAS and race yourself (RIVALS NONE for just the two of you). Against the rivals you must also be in a place by the end of each lap (7th, then 5th; over five laps 7th, 6th, 5th, 4th), or you are OUT: your place blinks while you are behind it (not in MODE LEARN, where you race them and are never put out).

    Guide: press GUIDE (or G) and the page reads the race out of the Game Boy's memory and shows what to press a moment before you need to: BRAKE before a bend you are too fast for, BOOST on a clear straight, NOSE DOWN or NOSE UP in the air, and which way to steer, round a barrier, off the dirt or onto the inside of a corner (and when to let go: the turn carries on). On a touch screen it lights the buttons too.

    Two players, two browsers: pair up first: open the LOBBY and press Race next to somebody's name, or press Invite a friend and send them the link. Once you are paired, the menu's MODE is set to LINK for you: both press GAS, and the race starts by itself when you are both waiting (2P). Whoever asked, or invited, hosts: the host's TRACK and RIVALS count for both. Each Game Boy runs its own car and two rivals and shows the other player's too (a few frames late, as a link cable between two consoles would), so each of you races five others; with RIVALS NONE it is just the two of you, heads up. LINK without a partner is an empty track. Keep the page in front: a tab in the background stops, and the link drops.

    Skin: (or SKIN, or the L key). LCD is the original Game Boy's green screen: its dots, their shadows and its slow liquid crystal, which smears what moves and half-lights what flickers. Smooth is the same without the lines between the dots; Classic the plain grey pixels.
